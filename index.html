Main Case Study

Designing a Guided Vehicle Photo Capture Flow

Vehicle photo capture is a critical part of the automotive listing and review process, but users can easily submit the wrong photo type, miss required shots, or provide low-quality images when the flow is unclear.


I worked on Photo Flow, a mobile-first guided capture experience that helps dealership users take the right vehicle photos quickly and consistently. My role focused on structuring the PRD, defining the core user flow, clarifying V1 and V2 scope, and translating business requirements into clear UX behaviours for design and development handoff.

Photo Flow Context

Vehicle photos are an important part of the automotive listing and review process. They help the business assess vehicle condition, ensure photo coverage, and maintain consistent listing quality. However, photo capture can easily become inconsistent when users are in a hurry or unsure what angle, object, or quality standard is expected. The business needed a flow that could guide users without slowing them down.

The flow starts from the vehicle record, guides users through camera-based capture, and allows them to review the completed media set before submission.

Problem Statement

The existing vehicle photo capture process created several operational risks. Users could take the wrong photo type, submit blurry or dark photos, miss mandatory shots, or require follow-up corrections after submission. These issues increased rework for the team and made it difficult to assess photo quality consistently at scale.

Design Challenge

The challenge was to balance speed and compliance: users needed a fast capture flow, while the business needed complete, high-quality, and reviewable photo evidence.

This project helped me understand how to design for operational workflows where users need to move quickly, but the business still requires consistent and compliant evidence. The key was not to slow users down with heavy instructions or hard blocking, but to guide them at the moment of action.

Overall, this project strengthened my ability to translate business requirements into practical UX behaviours and design decisions.

Research Approach

Understanding where users experience friction

The research explored where users may experience friction when accessing, understanding, and interacting with digital services. I looked across information clarity, accessibility, confidence, and representation to identify recurring barriers that could inform the design direction.

Methods: Interviews · Desk Research · Accessibility Review · Affinity Mapping

Information & clarity

How users understand dense, unfamiliar, or poorly structured information.

Accessibility

How visual, navigational, and interaction choices can create barriers for different access needs.

Confidence & decision-making

Where users may hesitate, feel uncertain, or need additional reassurance before taking action.

Representation & inclusion

How language, assumptions, and representation can make an experience feel inclusive or exclusionary.

Research observations were grouped into recurring themes around clarity, accessibility, confidence, and representation.

Key Insights

INSIGHT 01

Clarity is part of inclusion

Users benefit from clear language, visible structure, and predictable navigation. Reducing unnecessary complexity can make information easier to understand and help users feel more confident.

INSIGHT 02

Different users need different levels of support

Different users need different levels of guidance, information detail, and interaction support depending on their confidence, abilities, and context.

INSIGHT 03

Representation shapes trust

Language, imagery, and labels influence whether users feel recognised, respected, and included in the experience.

Inclusive Design Principles

These research insights were translated into three principles that guided the design direction.

PRINCIPLE 01

Clear & Accessible

Use clear language, readable content, predictable navigation, and accessible visual hierarchy to reduce unnecessary barriers.

PRINCIPLE 02

Flexible & Supportive

Provide different levels of guidance and support so users can interact with the experience according to their needs and confidence.

PRINCIPLE 03

Respectful & Inclusive

Avoid assumptions about users and use inclusive language, imagery, and representation to help more people feel recognised and respected.

Design Response

I translated the three design principles into practical interface responses across information structure, user guidance, and inclusive content.

01 — Clear & Accessible Information

I simplified the information hierarchy and strengthened readability so users can identify what matters, understand relationships between content, and find the next action more easily.

Clear hierarchy

Primary content and actions are prioritised through stronger visual structure.

Readable presentation

Spacing, typography, and contrast make dense content easier to scan.

Predictable actions

Consistent button states and content grouping make next steps easier to identify.

02 — Flexible & Supportive Guidance

I introduced contextual guidance at moments of uncertainty, giving users clearer explanations, feedback, and next steps without forcing everyone through the same level of support.

Contextual guidance

Helper text and explanations appear when additional support is useful.

Recovery support

Error states explain what happened and how users can continue.

Progress & reassurance

Progress and confirmation states help users understand where they are and what happens next.

I reviewed language, feedback, and representation to remove unnecessary assumptions and create a more respectful and inclusive experience.

Key Design Decisions

01 — Make content easier to scan

Research highlighted that dense information could quickly become difficult to process. I introduced shorter content blocks, stronger hierarchy and clearer grouping to reduce cognitive load.

02 — Give users more control

Rather than assuming one preferred way of accessing information, the experience provides flexible ways to navigate and engage with content.

03 — Design inclusion into the core experience

Accessibility was treated as part of the core interaction model rather than an additional setting added at the end.

Final Design

The final design focuses on clarity, accessibility, and supportive guidance. It aims to help users understand information more easily, complete tasks with confidence, and feel respected throughout the experience.

Outcome

The project resulted in a clearer and more inclusive design direction. By focusing on information hierarchy, accessibility, supportive guidance, and inclusive language, the design better supports users with different needs, confidence levels, and access conditions.

  • Identified key access and inclusion barriers

  • Improved clarity through stronger information hierarchy

  • Created a design direction that feels more respectful and supportive

Reflection

This project changed how I think about accessibility: rather than treating it as a final compliance check, I learned to use it as an input throughout the design process. Research also reinforced the importance of validating assumptions with users instead of designing around a single “default” experience.

Project Overview

ReSoil is a sustainability app concept developed during a 24-hour designathon. The project encourages users to reuse daily kitchen waste to cultivate organic soil, turning a small household action into a visible and rewarding habit.

My role focused on product concept development, user journey design, behaviour change strategy, UI design, and pitch storytelling. Our team developed the concept, prototype, and final presentation within 24 hours and achieved second place in the competition.

Design Context

Food waste is often treated as something to dispose of, rather than a resource that can be reused. Although many people are interested in sustainable living, it can be difficult to maintain eco-friendly habits when the action feels inconvenient, invisible, or disconnected from immediate reward.

ReSoil explores how a digital product can make kitchen waste reuse feel easier, more visible, and more motivating in everyday life.

03 / Make sustainability rewarding
